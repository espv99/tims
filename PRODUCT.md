# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Four audiences share the public site, mostly arriving on phones with limited data:
- Prospective students (bachilleres) comparing degrees: they want the professional profile, occupational field and curriculum.
- Current students: news, events, the student club and the student council.
- Faculty and authorities: institutional information, research and outreach projects, accreditation outcomes (RAE and educational objectives).
- Companies and the community: outreach (vinculación) projects, research and contact.

The admin CMS (news, events, teachers, users) is secondary and serves the department staff who publish content.

## Product Purpose

Official website of the Information Technology Engineering program (Ingeniería en Tecnologías de la Información) at ESPOCH, Sede Morona Santiago. It presents the program credibly to applicants and keeps the community informed through a combined news and events timeline. Success: an applicant understands what the degree is and trusts it within a minute, and a student finds what is happening without asking anyone.

## Brand Personality

Institutional and serious, close to its students, ambitious. Three words: credible, close, ambitious. The voice is clear Spanish (with English available), confident about outcomes, never stiff or bureaucratic. Brand colors already in use: deep navy and gold.

## Anti-references

- The generic university portal: stock-photo banners, carousels, logo walls, bureaucratic copy.
- The SaaS/startup template: gradients, identical icon cards, hero metrics.
- Cliché hacker aesthetics: green terminal text, neon, Matrix rain.

## Design Principles

1. Credibility first: structure, real content and restraint carry authority, not decoration.
2. Speak to the applicant, welcome the student: one page serves both without splitting into two sites.
3. Specific over generic: use the program's real projects, people and outcomes instead of filler.
4. Mobile and low bandwidth are the default: light assets, fast pages, readable on a small screen.
5. Bilingual by construction: layouts must hold both Spanish and English copy without breaking.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.1 AA as the minimum: contrast, keyboard navigation, screen reader support. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion`. Text must survive the Spanish/English switch and mobile widths without overflow.
